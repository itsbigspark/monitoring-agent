# Arc B — Stakeholder update

Per-slide guidance for an 8-slide stakeholder / board / SteerCo update.

## Slide 1 — Cover

**Intent**: Frame the period covered.

Title: "[Programme] · [Period]" — e.g., "Fraud Platform · Q2 2026
Update." Audience knows immediately what they're being briefed on and
for what time window.

Subhead in red: the headline (one sentence). Either "On track" or "At
risk" or "Decisions needed today" — set expectations honestly.

## Slide 2 — Headline metrics

**Intent**: Three numbers tell the audience whether this is good news.

Stats / Impact layout. Three numbers chosen to be:
- The most-important quantitative measure of progress.
- A risk or burn-rate measure (cost / time consumed / runway).
- A forward-looking measure (commit / pipeline / next milestone).

Numbers, not narratives. The first 30 seconds of the deck.

## Slide 3 — What changed since last update

**Intent**: Tell the story of the period in three beats.

3-Column Cards. Each card covers one beat of the period — what was
delivered, what was learned, what shifted. Beats are chronological by
default but can be thematic if the period had parallel workstreams.

What it must NOT be: a feature list. Cards are stories with
beginnings, middles, and ends.

## Slide 4 — Where we are vs the plan

**Intent**: Show position on the planned path.

Process Flow layout. The plan as a timeline; "you are here" marker
showing actual position. If actual deviates from planned, the
deviation is the story — don't bury it.

If actual matches planned, this slide can be a one-line confirmation
and you spend the time on slides 5–7.

## Slide 5 — Risks, dependencies, blockers

**Intent**: Surface what could derail us.

3-Column Cards. One card per risk dimension:
- Risks — internal things we're worried about.
- Dependencies — external things we're waiting on.
- Blockers — things actively stopping progress.

Be honest. Audience trusts updates that surface bad news; doesn't
trust updates that always say "on track."

## Slide 6 — Worked example

**Intent**: Tell one story in depth.

Case Study layout. Pick one delivery / decision / pivot from the
period and walk through it: context, what we did, what we learned,
what it cost.

Helps the audience calibrate the rest of the deck — they get a sense
of how the team actually operates by seeing one case in detail.

Optional but high-value. If pressed for time, cut this before
cutting risks (slide 5).

## Slide 7 — Decisions needed

**Intent**: Name the decisions you need from this audience.

3-Column Cards. Each card is one named decision:
- The decision (verb-noun, specific).
- Why it can't wait.
- What option you're recommending.

If you have no decisions to ask for, this slide should be deleted —
don't pad. But most useful updates do have one or two asks.

## Slide 8 — Closing / confirmation

**Intent**: Confirm the asks and the next checkpoint.

Closing layout. Three short paragraphs:
- "Asks today: [list them]" — call them back explicitly.
- "Next update: [date]" — set the next touchpoint.
- "Reach out: [name / email]" — leave the door open.

End with "Confirm or escalate today" not "Thanks."

---

## Cuts to short form (5 slides)

If the audience has 15 minutes not 30:

- **Cut 4** (where we are) — fold into slide 3.
- **Cut 6** (worked example) — speaker notes only.
- **Cut 7** (decisions) — fold the decisions into the closing slide.

Result: Cover → Headlines → What changed → Risks → Closing-with-asks.
